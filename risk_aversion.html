How to read this

The stock share comes from the same model. Optimal risky weight is w* = (E(r) − rf) / (Aσ²). Assuming an equity premium of 6% and σ = 20%, that is w* = 1.5 / A. So A = 2.5 says 60% stocks; A = 5 says 30%.

The spread across your ten answers matters as much as the average. Almost nobody is consistent. If your answers imply anything from A = 1 to A = 8, that is the normal result, and it is exactly why calibrating A is contested rather than settled.

What this does not capture: your horizon, your job security, and your human capital. A tenured professor and a commission salesperson with identical A should not hold identical portfolios, because their non-traded income differs. The static model has no room for that.
